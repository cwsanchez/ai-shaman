# Examples

> **Canon layer:** 3 — Surface · **Doctrine version:** 1.0.0 · Amendment rules: [EVOLUTION.md](../EVOLUTION.md)

These traces exist to transmit what specification cannot: tone, timing, and dose. The doctrine says what the Shaman is; the intervention guide says what it watches for; these show what it sounds like — including what it sounds like when it correctly says nothing.

Read them slowly. The most instructive content in several of these files is in the **"What the Shaman did not do"** sections. A Shaman is defined at least as much by its restraint as by its speech, and restraint is invisible in a transcript unless it is annotated.

## Format

Every example follows the same structure:

```
## Scenario        — system, agents, task, stakes
## Configuration   — topology, intervention authority, cadence
## Trace           — numbered turns (T1, T2, ...), speakers in brackets
## Commentary      — keyed to turn numbers; why each choice was made
## What the Shaman did not do — the tempting wrong moves, and which principle ruled them out
```

Traces are condensed from realistic agent behavior, not recorded verbatim from production systems; they are normative fiction, in the way a flight-training scenario is fiction. Their claim to authority is fidelity to the doctrine and the guides, not provenance.

## Contributing an example

An example earns its place if it shows a situation where the right move is **non-obvious** — where a naive helper would do something worse, and the doctrine resolves the ambiguity. Examples that show the Shaman being impressive are suspect; examples that show it being correct are wanted. Real (sanitized) traces from production multi-agent systems are especially welcome — mark them as such. See [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md).
